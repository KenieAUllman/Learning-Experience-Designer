01 • Challenge

The source material existed. The instruction didn’t.

A safety-critical operations team had extensive procedural documentation but no unified learning experience to prepare newly hired operational leaders for organization-specific responsibilities.

Subject-matter expertise lived across procedure manuals, operating standards, existing training, and experienced personnel. The design problem was not to summarize all of it. It was to determine what learners already knew, what was unique to the organization, what needed to be remembered, what needed to be practiced, and what belonged outside formal instruction.

06 • Facilitator Design

The facilitator is part of the learning experience.

Know Your Role

Purpose Introduce role clarity as the foundation of coordinated response.
Trainer Objective Learners should understand that effective response depends on people executing defined responsibilities rather than trying to solve every part independently.
Say High-pressure situations create a strong instinct to jump in. That can help, but it can also create confusion when responsibilities are unclear.
Ask What problems can happen when several people assume they own the same decision?
Listen For Conflicting instructions, duplicate work, missed responsibilities, poor accountability, delayed decisions.
Apply Three supervisors begin giving different instructions to the same frontline team. What problem exists before we even discuss the disruption itself?
Reinforce A coordinated response is not everyone doing everything. It is everyone understanding what they own.
Trainer Notes Keep the discussion focused on organizational role clarity, not real-world emergency procedure.

09 • Reflection

What the project reinforced.

High-stakes subject matter creates a strong temptation to teach everything.

This project reinforced the opposite lesson.

Effective instructional design requires defining the boundaries of instruction as deliberately as the content itself. Experienced learners didn’t need every foundational concept repeated. They needed organization-specific expectations, opportunities to reason through realistic situations, and a facilitator prepared to guide those conversations.

The value of the finished program wasn’t the number of procedures summarized or slides produced. It was the instructional structure connecting source material to decisions learners needed to make.
