About

I design learning by figuring out what people actually need first.

I started teaching while earning my graduate degree and quickly became fascinated by what happens when complicated ideas finally click.

That interest took me from education into corporate learning, consulting, and instructional design across technically complex and regulated environments.

What has stayed constant is the part I enjoy most: ambiguity.

Give me a complicated process, a stack of source material, an expert who knows so much they don't know where to begin, or a training program that isn't producing the outcome it should, and I want to understand why.

I tend to work from the system outward.

What should people be able to do? What prevents them from doing it today? What information actually matters? How should it be organized? What belongs in training? What belongs in performance support? And what doesn't need to be taught at all?

Then I build.

My background in education taught me how people learn. Work in regulated environments taught me to care about accuracy and execution. Consulting sharpened my ability to translate complexity into decisions. Data analysis taught me not to stop at whether someone completed a course.

I want to know whether the learning worked.

Professional Approach

Diagnose → Design → Measure

I use the framework quietly in the background to keep the work anchored to performance instead of drifting toward content for content’s sake.

01

Diagnose

Define the performance problem, audience, constraints, workflow, and evidence before deciding what learning should look like.

02

Design

Build the right combination of instruction, practice, facilitation, tools, and performance support around what people actually need to do.

03

Measure

Look beyond completion to determine whether the experience improved understanding, application, performance, or the larger system.
